\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented HealGen, a draft-first self-healing generation architecture
that couples a claim-level risk estimator, an evidence-sufficiency
predictor, conditional retrieval, a dynamic claim--evidence graph, and
localized repair behind one NLI judge --- and, more importantly, an
\emph{honest evaluation protocol} for such systems: frozen shared
evidence pools that no system can game by retrieving more, a gold-only
condition that separates gold-grounded support from retrieval-overlap
support, a pre-registered judge-validation gate, and scoring of the
\emph{emitted} answer rather than a system's private intermediate state.
On the HaluEval-QA benchmark at $N{=}\NTest$ with a shared
\texttt{flan-t5-base} generator, the protocol resolves real differences
between vanilla generation, always-on RAG, CoVe-style full-regeneration
verification, and HealGen's localized repair --- including the cost side
of the ledger (Table~\ref{tab:efficiency}) that single-metric leaderboards
hide.

The measurement itself surfaced findings a single-metric evaluation
would have missed: verification signals computed against system-private
evidence are structurally circular, and short-answer benchmarks collapse
the selective-retrieval branch of every adaptive system tested.  We
release the full pipeline, the frozen pools, the claim-level verdicts,
and the human spot-check instrument so both claims can be checked.

Future work runs the same protocol where the architecture has room to
demonstrate its selectivity --- long-form generation with multi-claim
answers --- and extends the judge stack (temperature scaling,
multi-judge agreement, human $\kappa$) once the spot-check is rated.
